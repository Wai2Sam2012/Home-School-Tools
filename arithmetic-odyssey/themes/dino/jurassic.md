# Top 20 Jurassic Dinosaurs

**Jurassic Period:** 201–145 million years ago (Ma).

The quick list gives an overview of all 20 dinosaurs. The [details](#dinosaur-details) below cover each one's type, period, where it was found, and interesting facts.

## Honorable mentions

Supersaurus, Dryosaurus, Anchiornis.

## Notes

- Archaeopteryx is usually classed as a bird, which makes it a dinosaur too.

## Dinosaur details

The details are based on each dinosaur's page on [dinosaurpictures.org](https://dinosaurpictures.org/jurassic-dinosaurs).

- **Type** is the dinosaur group from our list plus its diet.
- **Period**, **Found**, and **Facts** come from the site, except where a ✏ line says otherwise. The site's quick facts are generated from PaleoDB (the Paleobiology Database), so some of its dates, locations, and citations include fossils that were later reclassified.
- **✏ Corrected** lines mean the entry above it was fixed to match the mainstream scientific view, and they record what the site originally said.

---

### 1. Stegosaurus
- **Type:** Stegosaur · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~155–145 Ma
- **Found:** Western USA (Colorado, Wyoming, Utah) and Portugal
- **Facts:** 75 specimens are known. It laid eggs. The site's artwork shows a lone Stegosaurus swinging its spiked tail at an attacking theropod.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site extended its range into the Early Cretaceous, left out Portugal, and listed Xinjiang (China), which is a doubtful record.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Stegosaurus-pictures)

### 2. Brachiosaurus
- **Type:** Sauropod · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~161.2–145 Ma
- **Found:** Colorado and Utah (USA)
- **Facts:** It weighed about **29,000 kg**. 16 specimens are listed. It relied on its huge size for defense and lived in the Morrison Formation alongside Diplodocus.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site also listed Lindi (Tanzania), but the Tanzanian fossils are now placed in their own genus, Giraffatitan (#11). The site's "lived in a canyon" line is a template glitch and has been removed.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Brachiosaurus-pictures)

### 3. Allosaurus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~155–145 Ma
- **Found:** Western USA (Utah, Colorado, Wyoming) and Portugal
- **Facts:** The largest known specimen was about **32 ft** long. It weighed about 2,090 kg, and 117 specimens are known. It was long catalogued under the name *Antrodemus* before "Allosaurus" became the preferred name. The site notes that artwork often makes its arms too big.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's end date, "Coniacian Age," is a Late Cretaceous stage, which is far too late. Its location list (Utah, Germany, Nebraska) also left out Portugal, the home of *Allosaurus europaeus*.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Allosaurus-pictures)

### 4. Diplodocus
- **Type:** Sauropod · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic
- **Found:** Utah, New Mexico, and South Dakota (USA)
- **Facts:**
  - Its name means "double beam," after the bones under its tail.
  - The row of back spines often shown in artwork came from fossils that were later reassigned to Barosaurus.
  - Early artists drew it sprawling with its belly near the ground, but tracks and skeletons later showed it stood upright.
  - 60 specimens are known.
  - It weighed roughly **10–25 tonnes**, depending on the species.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site gave two different weights (~10,560 kg on this page and 25 tons on its Jurassic page). Both fit within the range for different Diplodocus species.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Diplodocus-pictures)

### 5. Apatosaurus / Brontosaurus
- **Type:** Sauropod · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic
- **Found:** Apatosaurus in Utah, Texas, and South Dakota; Brontosaurus in Wyoming and Colorado (USA)
- **Facts:**
  - Apatosaurus weighed about 19,500 kg. 51 specimens are known, compared with 21 for Brontosaurus.
  - It may have held its head upright to graze, like Brachiosaurus, rather than forward like Diplodocus.
  - The name "Brontosaurus" was long used for Apatosaurus, a mix-up that came out of the feud between the paleontologists Cope and Marsh. In **2015** Brontosaurus was shown to be a valid genus of its own.
- [Apatosaurus page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Apatosaurus-pictures) · [Brontosaurus page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Brontosaurus-pictures)

### 6. Archaeopteryx
- **Type:** Early bird / feathered theropod · Carnivore (small animals and insects)
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~150 Ma
- **Found:** Solnhofen area, Bavaria (Germany)
- **Facts:** It could fly and weighed only about **0.26 kg**. 10 specimens are known. Experts still argue about whether it glided between trees or flapped its wings and flew. Some class it as a dinosaur and some as an early bird.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's end date, "Barremian Age," is in the Early Cretaceous. It also listed Aragon (Spain) and Baden-Württemberg, which are doubtful records. All the classic Archaeopteryx fossils come from Late Jurassic Bavaria.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Archaeopteryx-pictures)

### 7. Dilophosaurus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Early Jurassic, ~199–183 Ma
- **Found:** Arizona (USA)
- **Facts:** It was about **6 m** long. Its two flat head crests were probably used for display or for controlling body heat, not as weapons. 6 specimens are known.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site also listed Telangana (India), a doubtful record, and said it "lived in a ridge," which is a template glitch. Both have been removed.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Dilophosaurus-pictures)

### 8. Ceratosaurus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~161.2 Ma to the Tithonian stage
- **Found:** Utah and Colorado (USA), Lindi (Tanzania)
- **Facts:** It weighed about 670 kg, and 16 specimens are known. The site's artwork shows one stalking a lungfish in a murky river.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Ceratosaurus-pictures)

### 9. Camarasaurus
- **Type:** Sauropod · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~155–145 Ma
- **Found:** Utah, Nebraska, and New Mexico (USA)
- **Facts:**
  - Its name means **"chambered lizard,"** after hollow spaces along its spine. These are now thought to have been air sacs connected to its breathing system, which would have made it lighter.
  - The innermost toe on each foot had a hooked claw.
  - It is the **most common sauropod found in North America**, with 121 specimens.
  - Most were about 15 m long. The largest species, *C. supremus*, may have reached about **23 m (75 ft)** and over 40 tonnes.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's end date, "Cenomanian Age," is in the Late Cretaceous, which is far too late. Its fact box also gave ~8,800 kg, which is well below most adult estimates.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Camarasaurus-pictures)

### 10. Megalosaurus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Middle Jurassic (Bathonian stage), ~166 Ma
- **Found:** Oxfordshire, England (UK)
- **Facts:** It was the **first dinosaur ever named**, by William Buckland in 1824. The site's oldest reference is an account from **1758** of a giant thigh-bone dug up at Stonesfield, England, long before anyone knew what dinosaurs were.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range ran into the Santonian (Late Cretaceous), and it listed France and Portugal with 59 specimens. That's because Megalosaurus was once used as a catch-all name for many unrelated fossils. The true Megalosaurus is from Middle Jurassic England.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Megalosaurus-pictures)

### 11. Giraffatitan
- **Type:** Sauropod · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic
- **Found:** Tendaguru, Lindi (Tanzania)
- **Facts:** It is the African "Brachiosaurus look-alike" from the famous Tendaguru dig site. It had close relatives in America's Morrison Formation.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Giraffatitan-pictures)

### 12. Compsognathus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore (also listed as an insectivore)
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~150 Ma
- **Found:** Europe (Germany and France)
- **Facts:** It weighed only about **2.5 kg**, and 4 specimens are known.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Compsognathus-pictures)

### 13. Kentrosaurus
- **Type:** Stegosaur · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic
- **Found:** Tendaguru, Lindi (Tanzania)
- **Facts:** It weighed about 321 kg, and 27 specimens are known. It was found during the Tendaguru expedition, which was written up in 1911.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Kentrosaurus-pictures)

### 14. Torvosaurus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic
- **Found:** Colorado (USA) and Portugal
- **Facts:** About 18 specimens are known. The site's artwork shows a large *Torvosaurus gurneyi* hunting fish in a coastal lagoon in ancient Portugal.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Torvosaurus-pictures)

### 15. Mamenchisaurus
- **Type:** Sauropod · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic
- **Found:** Sichuan and Chongqing (China); the site also lists Govi-Altai (Mongolia)
- **Facts:** 24 specimens are known. It lived on land and laid eggs.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's end date of 112 Ma is in the Early Cretaceous. Mamenchisaurus is a Jurassic sauropod.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Mamenchisaurus-pictures)

### 16. Guanlong
- **Type:** Early tyrannosaur relative (tyrannosauroid) · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Jurassic, ~163.5–157.3 Ma
- **Found:** Xinjiang (China)
- **Facts:** It was described by Xu & Clark in *Nature* (2006) as "a basal tyrannosauroid," making it an early relative of T. rex. The site's artwork shows one trapped in a muddy pit in the Shishugou marsh.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Guanlong-pictures)

### 17. Cryolophosaurus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Early Jurassic, ~199.3–182.7 Ma
- **Found:** **Antarctica**
- **Facts:** Only one specimen is known. It was described in 1993 in "A new Jurassic dinosaur fauna from Antarctica." The site's artwork imagines a male displaying its colorful crest to a rival.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Cryolophosaurus-pictures)

### 18. Scelidosaurus
- **Type:** Early armored dinosaur (thyreophoran) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Early Jurassic, ~191 Ma
- **Found:** Dorset, England (UK)
- **Facts:** 7 specimens are known. It is one of the earliest armored dinosaurs.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site listed Europe and North America plus Tibet (China), and a range starting in the Late Triassic. The well-known Scelidosaurus fossils all come from Early Jurassic Dorset.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Scelidosaurus-pictures)

### 19. Massospondylus
- **Type:** Sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Early Jurassic, ~200–183 Ma
- **Found:** South Africa, Lesotho, Botswana, and Zimbabwe
- **Facts:** It weighed about 137 kg, and **42 specimens** are known. The site's artwork shows it standing on two legs to reach high branches while a four-legged Pulanesaura feeds lower. The scene illustrates how Early Jurassic plant-eaters split up their food sources.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range started at 228 Ma, in the Late Triassic.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Massospondylus-pictures)

### 20. Heterodontosaurus
- **Type:** Ornithischian · Herbivore
- **Period:** Early Jurassic, ~200–190 Ma
- **Found:** South Africa
- **Facts:** It weighed only about **1.8 kg**, and 6 specimens are known. It was described in *Nature* in 1962, and a 2011 study described new specimens from southern Africa.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range started at 228 Ma (Triassic), and it also listed Mexico, which is a doubtful record.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Heterodontosaurus-pictures)
