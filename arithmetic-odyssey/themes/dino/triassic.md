# Top 20 Triassic Dinosaurs

**Triassic Period:** 252–201 million years ago (Ma). Dinosaurs first appear in the Late Triassic, so this list has fewer household names than the other periods.

The quick list gives an overview of all 20 dinosaurs. The [details](#dinosaur-details) below cover each one's type, period, where it was found, and interesting facts.

## Notes

- Scientists still debate whether Nyasasaurus and Pisanosaurus are true dinosaurs.
- Famous Triassic animals such as Postosuchus and the early pterosaurs are not dinosaurs, so they are left off this list.

## Dinosaur details

The details are based on each dinosaur's page on [dinosaurpictures.org](https://dinosaurpictures.org/triassic-dinosaurs).

- **Type** is the dinosaur group from our list plus its diet.
- **Period**, **Found**, and **Facts** come from the site, except where a ✏ line says otherwise. The site's quick facts are generated from PaleoDB (the Paleobiology Database), so some of its dates, locations, and citations include fossils that were later reclassified.
- **✏ Corrected** lines mean the entry above it was fixed to match the mainstream scientific view, and they record what the site originally said.

---

### 1. Eoraptor
- **Type:** Early saurischian · Omnivore
- **Period:** Triassic, ~237–228 million years ago (Ma)
- **Found:** Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** Only one specimen has ever been found. It was described by Sereno & Forster in *Nature* (1993). The site's artwork shows one hiding under ferns while the predator Saurosuchus walks past.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Eoraptor-pictures)

### 2. Herrerasaurus
- **Type:** Early theropod-like predator (herrerasaurid) · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic (Carnian stage), ~231 Ma
- **Found:** Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** 11 specimens are known. It lived on land and laid eggs. The key papers are Novas (1986) and Sereno & Forster (1993, *Nature*).
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range ran to 201.3 Ma, which is the end of the whole Triassic, not the age of the Herrerasaurus fossils.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Herrerasaurus-pictures)

### 3. Coelophysis
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic
- **Found:** Ghost Ranch, New Mexico (USA)
- **Facts:** It stood about 3 ft tall, was about 9 ft long, and weighed around 30 lb (~15 kg). It had the **first wishbone known to paleontology** and small but useful forelimbs. The site describes it as a sharp-eyed, fast-running hunter of small game with excellent depth perception.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site also listed the Jurassic and Africa because it counts a related species from Zimbabwe. That species is now often placed in its own genus, *Megapnosaurus*.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Coelophysis-pictures)

### 4. Plateosaurus
- **Type:** Sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic (Norian stage), ~214–204 Ma
- **Found:** Germany, Switzerland, and France; also Greenland
- **Facts:** 33 specimens are known, which makes it one of the best-represented Triassic dinosaurs. Predators on other pages of the site are often shown hunting Plateosaurus.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site gave a much wider range of 237–199.3 Ma and listed only Germany and Greenland.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Plateosaurus-pictures)

### 5. Staurikosaurus
- **Type:** Herrerasaurid · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic (Carnian stage), ~233 Ma
- **Found:** Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil (South America)
- **Facts:** Only 2 specimens are known. It was described by Colbert in 1970. The site's artwork shows it chasing a rhynchosaur (a beaked reptile) across a dusty floodplain.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range ran to ~208.5 Ma, which is later than the Carnian rocks it was found in.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Staurikosaurus-pictures)

### 6. Nyasasaurus
- **Type:** Possible dinosaur · Diet unknown
- **Period:** **Middle** Triassic, ~247–242 Ma, the oldest on this list
- **Found:** Tanzania (Africa)
- **Facts:** 2 specimens are known. The fossils were first studied in Charig's 1956 dissertation but were not formally named until **2013** (Nesbitt et al.). Scientists still debate whether it is a true dinosaur or a very close relative.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site listed it as a herbivore, but its diet is not known from the fossils, which are mostly arm and backbone material.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Nyasasaurus-pictures)

### 7. Eodromaeus
- **Type:** Early theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Triassic, ~237–228 Ma
- **Found:** Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** Only one specimen is known. It laid eggs. It was described by Martinez & Sereno in *Science* (2011) as "a basal dinosaur from the dawn of the dinosaur era." The site's artwork shows it sprinting through ferns after a lizard.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Eodromaeus-pictures)

### 8. Saturnalia
- **Type:** Early sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Triassic, ~237–208.5 Ma
- **Found:** Rio Grande do Sul (Brazil) and Mashonaland West (Zimbabwe)
- **Facts:** 3 specimens are known. It was described in 1999 as a sauropodomorph from southern Brazil. The site's artwork shows it watching a Staurikosaurus at a riverbed.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Saturnalia-pictures)

### 9. Buriolestes
- **Type:** Early sauropodomorph · **Carnivore**
- **Period:** Late Triassic (Carnian stage), ~233 Ma
- **Found:** Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil (South America)
- **Facts:** It was described in **2016** (Cabreira et al.). It is famous because it was a **meat-eating** early relative of the giant plant-eating sauropods, which shows that the long-neck line started out as predators.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site listed it as a herbivore and cited a 2014 *Zootaxa* paper about an aetosaur, an armored reptile that is not a dinosaur.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Buriolestes-pictures)

### 10. Panphagia
- **Type:** Early sauropodomorph · Omnivore
- **Period:** Triassic, ~237–228 Ma
- **Found:** Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** Only one specimen is known. It was described by Martínez & Alcober in *PLoS ONE* (2009) as a basal sauropodomorph from the Ischigualasto Formation. The site's artwork shows it catching a beetle in a fern thicket, which fits its "eats everything" name.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Panphagia-pictures)

### 11. Pisanosaurus
- **Type:** Possible early ornithischian · Herbivore
- **Period:** Triassic, ~237–228 Ma
- **Found:** Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** Only one specimen is known. It was described by Casamiquela (1967). The site's artwork shows it fleeing a Herrerasaurus.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Pisanosaurus-pictures)

### 12. Liliensternus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic (Norian to Rhaetian stages)
- **Found:** Bavaria and Thuringia (Germany), Luzern (Switzerland)
- **Facts:** 5 specimens are known. It lived on land and laid eggs. The site's references go back to an 1837 paper by H. v. Meyer.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Liliensternus-pictures)

### 13. Tawa
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic
- **Found:** New Mexico (USA)
- **Facts:** It is known from a **complete** Late Triassic skeleton, described in *Science* (2009), which shed light on early dinosaur evolution. The site's artwork shows it chasing a gliding lizard through horsetail plants.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Tawa-pictures)

### 14. Chindesaurus
- **Type:** Herrerasaurid · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic, ~228–208.5 Ma
- **Found:** Petrified Forest National Park, **Arizona** (USA), where the original fossil came from; also New Mexico and Texas
- **Facts:** 7 specimens are known. It lived on land and laid eggs. It is cited in Irmis & Nesbitt's 2007 *Science* paper on Late Triassic dinosaur relatives from New Mexico.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site left out Arizona and listed only New Mexico and Texas.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Chindesaurus-pictures)

### 15. Daemonosaurus
- **Type:** Theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic, ~208.5–201.3 Ma
- **Found:** Ghost Ranch, New Mexico (USA)
- **Facts:** It is known from only one specimen, a skull with buck-toothed front teeth. It was described in **2011** (Sues et al.).
- **✏ Corrected:** The site cited Colbert's 1947 article "The little dinosaurs of Ghost Ranch," which is about Coelophysis and was written 64 years before Daemonosaurus was named.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Daemonosaurus-pictures)

### 16. Riojasaurus
- **Type:** Sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic, ~228–208.5 Ma
- **Found:** Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** 2 specimens are known. Its first skull and jaws were described by Bonaparte & Pumares (1995). The site's artwork shows a bull Riojasaurus rearing up on its hind legs.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Riojasaurus-pictures)

### 17. Melanorosaurus
- **Type:** Sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic
- **Found:** Free State and Eastern Cape (South Africa)
- **Facts:** About 10 specimens are known. It was described in papers from 1957 and 1984.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range ran to 190.8 Ma, which is in the Early Jurassic and contradicts its own "Triassic" label. The site also said it "lived in a mountain," which is a template glitch and has been removed.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Melanorosaurus-pictures)

### 18. Thecodontosaurus
- **Type:** Sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic
- **Found:** Near Bristol, England (UK)
- **Facts:** It was found in 1834, **eight years before the word "dinosaur" was invented**, and named in 1836. 14 specimens are known.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site gave a range of 247.2 Ma to the Early Jurassic and also listed Bavaria and Zimbabwe. Those extras come from older fossils that were once lumped in with it. The site also cited an 1824 Buckland work, which predates the 1834 discovery.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Thecodontosaurus-pictures)

### 19. Efraasia
- **Type:** Sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic (Norian stage)
- **Found:** Baden-Württemberg (Germany)
- **Facts:** 5 specimens are known. It was described in German papers from 1907 and 1938. The site's artwork shows it reaching for a conifer branch.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site listed Grand Est (France). The main Efraasia fossils come from Baden-Württemberg in Germany.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Efraasia-pictures)

### 20. Ingentia
- **Type:** Sauropodomorph · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Triassic
- **Found:** San Juan Province, Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** It was described in **2018** (Apaldetti et al.) as an early giant, about 8–10 m long. It lived well before the true sauropods, which shows that giant size evolved earlier than once thought.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site cited a 2013 paper about a sphenodontian (a lizard-like reptile), not Ingentia.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Ingentia-pictures)
