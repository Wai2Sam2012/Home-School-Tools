# Top 20 Cretaceous Dinosaurs

**Cretaceous Period:** 145–66 million years ago (Ma).

The quick list gives an overview of all 20 dinosaurs. The [details](#dinosaur-details) below cover each one's type, period, where it was found, and interesting facts.

## Quick list

| # | Dinosaur | Epoch | Where found | Why it's notable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tyrannosaurus rex | Late | USA, Canada | The most famous predator |
| 2 | Triceratops | Late | USA, Canada | Three horns and a frill |
| 3 | Velociraptor | Late | Mongolia | Feathered, turkey-sized raptor |
| 4 | Spinosaurus | Mid–Late | North Africa | Sail-backed, semi-aquatic fish-eater |
| 5 | Ankylosaurus | Late | USA, Canada | Armored body with a tail club |
| 6 | Iguanodon | Early | Europe | Thumb spike; second dinosaur named (1825) |
| 7 | Parasaurolophus | Late | USA, Canada | Tube crest used to make sounds |
| 8 | Pachycephalosaurus | Late | USA | Dome-headed |
| 9 | Giganotosaurus | Late | Argentina | Rivaled T. rex in size |
| 10 | Argentinosaurus | Late | Argentina | One of the largest land animals ever |
| 11 | Carnotaurus | Late | Argentina | Bull-like horns and tiny arms |
| 12 | Deinonychus | Early | USA | Sparked the "dinosaur renaissance" |
| 13 | Utahraptor | Early | USA (Utah) | Largest known raptor (~6 m) |
| 14 | Therizinosaurus | Late | Mongolia | Claws up to ~1 m long |
| 15 | Edmontosaurus | Late | USA, Canada | Duck-billed; "mummy" fossils with skin |
| 16 | Protoceratops | Late | Mongolia | Found locked in combat with Velociraptor |
| 17 | Oviraptor | Late | Mongolia | Wrongly called an "egg thief" (was guarding its own nest) |
| 18 | Microraptor | Early | China | Four-winged gliding dinosaur |
| 19 | Carcharodontosaurus | Late | North Africa | "Shark-toothed" giant predator |
| 20 | Baryonyx | Early | England | Fish-eater with a large thumb claw |

## Honorable mentions

Maiasaura, Styracosaurus, Gallimimus, Albertosaurus, Psittacosaurus, Yutyrannus.

## Notes

- Spinosaurus's exact lifestyle (how much time it spent in water) is still debated.

## Dinosaur details

The details are based on each dinosaur's page on [dinosaurpictures.org](https://dinosaurpictures.org/cretaceous-dinosaurs).

- **Type** is the dinosaur group from our list plus its diet.
- **Period**, **Found**, and **Facts** come from the site, except where a ✏ line says otherwise. The site's quick facts are generated from PaleoDB (the Paleobiology Database), so some of its dates, locations, and citations include fossils that were later reclassified.
- **✏ Corrected** lines mean the entry above it was fixed to match the mainstream scientific view, and they record what the site originally said.

---

### 1. Tyrannosaurus rex
- **Type:** Theropod (tyrannosaur) · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~68–66 Ma
- **Found:** Montana and Utah (USA), Coahuila (Mexico)
- **Facts:** The largest complete skeleton is **40 ft** long. It weighed about 7,224 kg, and 81 specimens are known. Fossil skin impressions published in 2017 suggest adults were **mostly scaly**. Whether it had any feathers at all is still debated.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site said it was "increasingly likely" that T. rex had feathers or filaments over much of its body. Current skin evidence points the other way for adults.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Tyrannosaurus-pictures)

### 2. Triceratops
- **Type:** Ceratopsian (horned dinosaur) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~68–66 Ma, the very end of the age of dinosaurs
- **Found:** North Dakota and Wyoming (USA), Saskatchewan (Canada)
- **Facts:** It weighed about **3,938 kg**. **139 specimens** are known, the most of any dinosaur in this list. The site's artwork shows an adult facing off against a T. rex.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range started at 83.5 Ma, which is about 15 million years too early. It also said it "lived in a mountain," which is a template glitch and has been removed.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Triceratops-pictures)

### 3. Velociraptor
- **Type:** Dromaeosaur (raptor) · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~75–71 Ma
- **Found:** Mongolia and Inner Mongolia (China)
- **Facts:**
  - It had a sickle-shaped claw on each foot and a long tail for balance.
  - It was **feathered**, which the site says gave it "the appearance of a vicious primordial turkey."
  - It was turkey-sized at about **15–20 kg**, much smaller than the movie versions, which look more like Deinonychus or Utahraptor.
  - It appears in the famous "Fighting Dinosaurs" fossil, locked in combat with a Protoceratops.
  - 18 specimens are listed.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's fact box gave ~45 kg, which is too heavy for a turkey-sized animal. It also listed Alberta (Canada) and Batken (Kyrgyzstan), which are doubtful records.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Velociraptor-pictures)

### 4. Spinosaurus
- **Type:** Spinosaurid theropod · Carnivore (mostly fish)
- **Period:** Cretaceous
- **Found:** Tataouine (Tunisia), Adrar and Béchar (Algeria)
- **Facts:** 21 specimens are listed. The site cites a 2005 study of its skull and size. Its artwork shows it fully underwater, swimming with its **"newly-discovered paddle tail."**
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Spinosaurus-pictures)

### 5. Ankylosaurus
- **Type:** Ankylosaur (armored dinosaur) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous
- **Found:** Montana and Colorado (USA), Alberta (Canada)
- **Facts:** About 14 specimens are known. It laid eggs. The site's artwork shows a tail-club standoff with young tyrannosaurs.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Ankylosaurus-pictures)

### 6. Iguanodon
- **Type:** Ornithopod · Herbivore
- **Period:** Early Cretaceous, ~126–122 Ma
- **Found:** Belgium, England, Germany, Spain, and France
- **Facts:** **123 specimens** are known. The site cites an 1878 paper on the fossils found in a Belgian coal mine. That was the Bernissart find of dozens of Iguanodon skeletons.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range started in the Kimmeridgian (Late Jurassic), probably because older fossils were once lumped into Iguanodon. Its location list (Romania, Spain, France) also left out Belgium, which is where the most famous fossils come from.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Iguanodon-pictures)

### 7. Parasaurolophus
- **Type:** Hadrosaur (duck-billed dinosaur) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~77–73 Ma
- **Found:** Alberta (Canada), New Mexico and Utah (USA)
- **Facts:** It weighed about **2.5 tonnes**. 17 specimens are known. It laid eggs. The site cites a 1979 study of a specimen from Utah.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site gave a weight of ~5,057 kg, about double most estimates, and a range of 83.5–70.6 Ma. It listed Arizona instead of Utah, even though its own cited specimen is from Utah.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Parasaurolophus-pictures)

### 8. Pachycephalosaurus
- **Type:** Pachycephalosaur (dome-headed dinosaur) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous (Campanian to Maastrichtian stages)
- **Found:** South Dakota, Colorado, and Montana (USA)
- **Facts:** 30 specimens are known. Some researchers think the dinosaurs named **Dracorex and Stygimoloch may just be younger Pachycephalosaurus** at different growth stages, rather than separate genera.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Pachycephalosaurus-pictures)

### 9. Giganotosaurus
- **Type:** Carcharodontosaurid theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian stage), ~99–97 Ma
- **Found:** Patagonia, Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** It was about **40 ft (12–13 m)** long and weighed about **6–8 tonnes**, roughly T. rex's size. 3 specimens are known. It was described by Coria & Salgado in 1994 as "a giant theropod from the middle Cretaceous of Patagonia." The site's artwork shows two adults closing in on a sauropod, Andesaurus, stuck in mud.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's South America page gave a weight of nearly 15 tons, about double most estimates. Its fact-box figure of ~6,000 kg is close to the accepted range.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Giganotosaurus-pictures)

### 10. Argentinosaurus
- **Type:** Titanosaur (sauropod) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~99.6 Ma to the Turonian stage
- **Found:** Neuquén Province, Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** It is known from **only one specimen**, discovered in 1987. The site estimates it at about **100 ft long and 80 tons**. It was described by Bonaparte & Coria in 1993 as a "new and huge titanosaur."
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Argentinosaurus-pictures)

### 11. Carnotaurus
- **Type:** Abelisaurid theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~72–69 Ma
- **Found:** Patagonia, Argentina (South America)
- **Facts:** It is known from only one specimen. Its name means **"flesh-eating bull,"** after the two thick horns above its eyes. It was about 25 ft long and weighed about 1.3 tons. It was described by Bonaparte in 1985.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site called it "the only dinosaur with horns," which is wrong. Triceratops and Ceratosaurus had horns too. What stands out about Carnotaurus is that it was a meat-eater with bull-like brow horns.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Carnotaurus-pictures)

### 12. Deinonychus
- **Type:** Dromaeosaur (raptor) · Carnivore
- **Period:** Early Cretaceous, ~115–108 Ma
- **Found:** Montana, Wyoming, and Colorado (USA)
- **Facts:** It weighed about 75 kg, and 41 specimens are known. The site cites Ostrom's 1976 description and a 2010 bite-force study based on bite marks. The site's artwork shows two Deinonychus attacking a young Tenontosaurus.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site gave a range of 145–100.5 Ma, which covers the entire Early Cretaceous. Deinonychus lived in the later part of it.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Deinonychus-pictures)

### 13. Utahraptor
- **Type:** Dromaeosaur (raptor) · Carnivore
- **Period:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian to Aptian stages)
- **Found:** Utah (USA)
- **Facts:** 2 specimens are listed. It was described by Kirkland and colleagues in 1993. Other pages on the site call it the **apex predator** of its region.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Utahraptor-pictures)

### 14. Therizinosaurus
- **Type:** Therizinosaur (plant-eating theropod) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian stage, ending 66 Ma)
- **Found:** Ömnögovi and Bayankhongor (Mongolia)
- **Facts:** 2 specimens are known. It was described by Maleev in 1954. It is a **plant-eating member of the meat-eating theropod group**.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Therizinosaurus-pictures)

### 15. Edmontosaurus
- **Type:** Hadrosaur (duck-billed dinosaur) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous (ending 66 Ma)
- **Found:** South Dakota, Wyoming, and Montana (USA), Alberta (Canada)
- **Facts:**
  - The site describes it as "more duck-billed than most," with a face like a shovel at the end of a long neck.
  - The first fossils came from Hell Creek in South Dakota and from Wyoming in the 1800s.
  - Its name has been used since 1917, after a find in the Edmonton Formation in Canada.
  - **71 specimens** are known.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's "lived in a canyon" line is a template glitch and has been removed.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Edmontosaurus-pictures)

### 16. Protoceratops
- **Type:** Ceratopsian (horned dinosaur) · Herbivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~75–71 Ma
- **Found:** Gobi Desert: Bayankhongor (Mongolia), Inner Mongolia and Gansu (China)
- **Facts:** It was sheep-sized, at about **60–100 kg**. 37 specimens are known. The site's artwork recreates the **"Fighting Dinosaurs"** fossil, with Protoceratops biting down on an attacking Velociraptor.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site gave a range of 145–66 Ma, which covers the whole Cretaceous, and a weight of ~177 kg, which is above most estimates.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Protoceratops-pictures)

### 17. Oviraptor
- **Type:** Oviraptorosaur · Omnivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous, ~75–71 Ma
- **Found:** Ömnögovi (Mongolia)
- **Facts:** Only 2 specimens are known. Its name means "egg thief," but as our list notes, it was actually guarding its own nest.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's range started in the Coniacian (~89 Ma), and it also listed Batken (Kyrgyzstan), a doubtful record.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Oviraptor-pictures)

### 18. Microraptor
- **Type:** Dromaeosaur (four-winged raptor) · Carnivore
- **Period:** Early Cretaceous (~125–120 Ma)
- **Found:** Liaoning, Hebei, and Inner Mongolia (China)
- **Facts:** 10 specimens are known. It was described in studies from 2002, 2007, and 2011.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Microraptor-pictures)

### 19. Carcharodontosaurus
- **Type:** Carcharodontosaurid theropod · Carnivore
- **Period:** Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian stage), ~100–94 Ma
- **Found:** Egypt (the original fossils), Morocco, Algeria, and Tunisia
- **Facts:** Its name means **"shark-toothed lizard,"** because its teeth look like those of the great white shark (*Carcharodon*). The site estimates it at about 40 ft long and 12 tons. 35 specimens are known. It was described by Stromer in 1931. The original Egyptian fossils were **destroyed in a WWII bombing raid** in 1944. The site's artwork shows it facing off against a Spinosaurus on a riverbank.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site's pages disagreed about where it was found (Egypt on one, Algeria and Tunisia on another). Both are right. The site's date also just said "from 145 Ma," which is the start of the whole Cretaceous.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Carcharodontosaurus-pictures)

### 20. Baryonyx
- **Type:** Spinosaurid theropod · Carnivore (mostly fish)
- **Period:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian stage), ~130–125 Ma
- **Found:** Surrey, England (UK); also La Rioja and Castile and León (Spain)
- **Facts:** Its name is Latin for **"heavy claw."** It had a crocodile-like head and a small skull for a theropod, with a long tail for balance. It is often shown catching fish with its claws, and the site's artwork shows it hunting a *Lepidotes* fish. 9 specimens are known.
- **✏ Corrected:** The site also listed Africa. The African fish-eater sometimes linked to it is a separate genus, *Suchomimus*. The site's "lived in a clay" line is a template glitch and has been removed.
- [Site page](https://dinosaurpictures.org/Baryonyx-pictures)
