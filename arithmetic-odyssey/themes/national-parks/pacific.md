## National Park of American Samoa

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | American Samoa (US territory) |
| **Became a national park** | October 31, 1988 (officially the National Park of American Samoa) |
| **Size** | about 8,300 acres (13 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 43,000 (58th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **South of the equator:** The only US national park south of the equator.
- **Coral reefs:** Some of the healthiest reefs in US waters, with excellent snorkeling at Ofu Beach.
- **Rainforest:** Old-world tropical rainforest that covers steep volcanic mountains.
- **Flying foxes:** Large fruit bats that fly during the day.

**History:** The park protects land on the islands of Tutuila, Taʻū, Ofu and Olosega. It does not own the land: it leases it from Samoan villages under a 50-year agreement signed in 1993, so that fa'a Samoa, the traditional Samoan way of life, continues. The park was first proposed to protect the rainforest and the flying foxes that pollinate it.

**Good to know:** Flights come through Honolulu. Ask permission before entering villages, and dress modestly.

## Crater Lake National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Oregon |
| **Became a national park** | May 22, 1902 |
| **Size** | about 183,000 acres (290 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 632,000 (36th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Crater Lake:** The deepest lake in the United States (1,943 ft), famous for its intense blue color.
- **Rim Drive:** A 33-mile road around the caldera rim with many viewpoints.
- **Wizard Island and Phantom Ship:** A cinder cone island and a rocky island that looks like a ghost ship.
- **Cleetwood Cove Trail:** The only trail to the lake shore, where boat tours leave for Wizard Island.

**History:** About 7,700 years ago the volcano Mount Mazama erupted and collapsed, forming a basin that filled with rain and snowmelt. The Klamath people witnessed the eruption and tell of it in their stories. William Gladstone Steel campaigned for 17 years to protect the lake, and it became one of the country's oldest national parks in 1902.

**Good to know:** The park gets about 42 ft of snow a year. Rim Drive is usually fully open only from July to October.

## Haleakalā National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Hawaii (Maui) |
| **Became a national park** | July 1, 1961 (part of Hawaii National Park from 1916) |
| **Size** | about 33,300 acres (52 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 854,000 (27th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Sunrise at the summit:** Watching the sun rise above the clouds from the 10,023-ft summit.
- **Haleakalā crater:** A huge valley on the summit with cinder cones and trails such as Sliding Sands.
- **Silversword and nēnē:** A rare silver plant found almost nowhere else, and the nēnē, Hawaii's state bird.
- **Kīpahulu:** On the coast, the Pīpīwai Trail runs through a bamboo forest to the 400-ft Waimoku Falls.

**History:** Haleakalā, "house of the sun," is a sacred place for Native Hawaiians. In legend, the demigod Māui snared the sun here to slow its path across the sky. It became part of Hawaii National Park in 1916 and a separate national park in 1961.

**Good to know:** Sunrise visits need a reservation. The summit is cold, often near freezing before dawn.

## Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Hawaii (Hawaiʻi Island) |
| **Became a national park** | August 1, 1916 |
| **Size** | about 354,000 acres (550 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.88 million (18th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Kīlauea:** One of the most active volcanoes on Earth, with frequent eruptions in its summit crater, Halemaʻumaʻu.
- **Mauna Loa:** The largest active volcano on Earth, which last erupted in 2022.
- **Chain of Craters Road:** A road that drops 3,700 ft to the coast past craters and lava flows.
- **Nāhuku lava tube:** A walk through a tunnel formed by flowing lava.

**History:** Kīlauea is the home of Pele, the Hawaiian goddess of fire and volcanoes, and remains a sacred place. The park was created in 1916, along with Haleakalā, as Hawaii National Park. In 2018 an eruption drained Kīlauea's summit lava lake and collapsed the crater, closing much of the park for months. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

**Good to know:** Eruptions come and go. Check the park and USGS websites for current lava viewing and volcanic smog (vog).

## Mount Rainier National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Washington |
| **Became a national park** | March 2, 1899 |
| **Size** | about 236,000 acres (370 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.64 million (20th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Mount Rainier:** An active volcano (14,410 ft) and the most glaciated peak in the contiguous US.
- **Paradise:** Wildflower meadows in summer and one of the snowiest places on Earth in winter.
- **Sunrise:** The highest point in the park reachable by car (6,400 ft), with close views of the mountain.
- **Wonderland Trail:** A 93-mile trail that circles the whole mountain.

**History:** The Nisqually, Puyallup, Muckleshoot, Yakama and Cowlitz peoples call the mountain Tahoma or Takhoma. It became the fifth national park in 1899. Its roads and rustic buildings, built in the 1920s and 1930s, are now a National Historic Landmark District.

**Good to know:** Summer timed-entry reservations have been needed in recent years. Check the park website before visiting.

## North Cascades National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Washington |
| **Became a national park** | October 2, 1968 (managed with Ross Lake and Lake Chelan national recreation areas) |
| **Size** | about 505,000 acres (790 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 47,000 (57th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Glaciers:** More than 300 glaciers, the most of any US national park outside Alaska.
- **The "American Alps":** Jagged, rugged peaks, best seen from trails like Cascade Pass.
- **Diablo Lake:** A bright turquoise lake colored by glacial silt, next to the North Cascades Highway.
- **Stehekin:** A remote village reachable only by boat, plane or on foot.

**History:** The Upper Skagit, Sauk-Suiattle and other Coast Salish peoples, and the Nlaka'pamux, used the mountains and passes for thousands of years. Seattle City Light built dams on the Skagit River from the 1920s to the 1960s. The park was created in 1968 together with two neighboring recreation areas. Its visitor count looks low because most people stay along the highway, which is in the recreation area rather than the park.

**Good to know:** The North Cascades Highway (State Route 20) closes in winter, usually from November to April or May.

## Olympic National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Washington |
| **Became a national park** | June 29, 1938 (Mount Olympus National Monument from 1909) |
| **Size** | about 923,000 acres (1,440 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 3.58 million (9th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Three parks in one:** Glacier-capped mountains, temperate rainforests and more than 70 miles of wild Pacific coast.
- **Hoh Rain Forest:** Moss-draped trees in one of the wettest places in the contiguous US.
- **Wild beaches:** Sea stacks and tide pools at Ruby Beach, Rialto Beach and Hole-in-the-Wall.
- **Hurricane Ridge and Lake Crescent:** Mountain views and wildflower meadows, and a deep, clear glacier-carved lake.

**History:** The Makah, Quileute, Hoh, Quinault and other tribes have lived on the peninsula for thousands of years. President Theodore Roosevelt created Mount Olympus National Monument in 1909 to protect its Roosevelt elk, and President Franklin Roosevelt made it a national park in 1938. From 2011 to 2014, two dams on the Elwha River were removed, the largest dam removal in US history at the time, and salmon returned to the river. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

**Good to know:** No road crosses the park; Highway 101 loops around it. Plan for rain at any time of year.
