## Arches National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Utah |
| **Became a national park** | November 12, 1971 (national monument from 1929) |
| **Size** | about 76,700 acres (120 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.51 million (21st most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Over 2,000 arches:** The greatest concentration of natural stone arches in the world.
- **Delicate Arch:** A free-standing arch shown on Utah license plates, reached by a 3-mile round-trip hike.
- **Landscape Arch:** One of the longest natural arches in the world, about 306 ft across.
- **Windows and Balanced Rock:** Easy walks to Double Arch, the North and South Windows and a huge balanced boulder.

**History:** Ancestral Puebloan and Fremont peoples, and later the Ute and Paiute, lived in the area. Rancher John Wesley Wolfe homesteaded near Delicate Arch in 1898. Writer Edward Abbey worked here as a ranger in the 1950s and wrote "Desert Solitaire" about it. It became a national monument in 1929 and a national park in 1971.

**Good to know:** Timed-entry reservations have been needed in the busy season in recent years. The town of Moab is next to the park.

## Big Bend National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Texas |
| **Became a national park** | June 12, 1944 |
| **Size** | about 801,000 acres (1,250 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 568,000 (38th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Chisos Mountains:** The only mountain range entirely inside a US national park, with trails such as Lost Mine and the South Rim.
- **Santa Elena Canyon:** 1,500-ft limestone walls rising straight up from the Rio Grande.
- **Rio Grande:** Canoe and raft trips along the US–Mexico border, plus hot springs on the riverbank.
- **Birds and dark skies:** More than 450 bird species and some of the darkest skies in the lower 48 states.

**History:** The Chisos, Jumano, Mescalero Apache and Comanche peoples lived in or passed through the area. Ranching and mining towns grew up in the late 1800s. Texas bought the land and gave it to the federal government, and it became a national park in 1944.

**Good to know:** The park is very remote. Visit from November to April; summers are extremely hot.

## Bryce Canyon National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Utah |
| **Became a national park** | February 25, 1928 (national monument from 1923) |
| **Size** | about 35,800 acres (56 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.97 million (16th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Hoodoos:** The largest collection of hoodoos (tall, thin rock spires) in the world.
- **Bryce Amphitheater:** Viewpoints at Sunrise, Sunset, Inspiration and Bryce Points.
- **Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden:** Trails that wind among the hoodoos, past Thor's Hammer and through Wall Street.
- **Dark skies:** Very dark night skies and an annual astronomy festival.

**History:** In Paiute stories, the hoodoos are the "Legend People" who were turned to stone. The park is named after Ebenezer Bryce, a Mormon settler who is said to have called it "a hell of a place to lose a cow." It is not really a canyon but a series of natural amphitheaters. It became a national monument in 1923 and a national park in 1928.

**Good to know:** The park sits at 8,000–9,000 ft, so nights are cold and winters are snowy. A free shuttle runs in summer.

## Canyonlands National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Utah |
| **Became a national park** | September 12, 1964 |
| **Size** | about 337,600 acres (530 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 796,000 (29th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Island in the Sky:** A high mesa with Mesa Arch at sunrise and Grand View Point.
- **The Needles:** Colorful sandstone spires and backpacking routes such as Chesler Park.
- **The Maze and the rivers:** One of the most remote places in the US, and the Green and Colorado rivers, with Cataract Canyon's rapids.
- **Great Gallery:** Life-size ancient rock paintings in Horseshoe Canyon.

**History:** Ancestral Puebloan and Fremont peoples left rock art and granaries here. John Wesley Powell's expedition floated through in 1869. Roads cut during the 1950s uranium boom opened up the area, and Arches superintendent Bates Wilson, called the "father of Canyonlands," campaigned for the park, created in 1964.

**Good to know:** The districts are not connected by roads inside the park. Moab is the base for Island in the Sky.

## Capitol Reef National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Utah |
| **Became a national park** | December 18, 1971 (national monument from 1937) |
| **Size** | about 241,900 acres (380 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.39 million (22nd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Waterpocket Fold:** A wrinkle in the Earth's crust nearly 100 miles long.
- **Fruita orchards:** Historic fruit orchards where you can pick fruit in season, and fresh pies at the Gifford Homestead.
- **Petroglyphs and Hickman Bridge:** Fremont rock art and a natural bridge reached by a short hike.
- **Cathedral Valley:** Huge sandstone monoliths, such as the Temples of the Sun and Moon, in a remote part of the park.

**History:** The Fremont people lived here about 1,000 years ago. Mormon pioneers settled the oasis of Fruita in the 1880s and planted orchards. The name comes from white rock domes that look like the US Capitol and the cliffs that formed a "reef" blocking travel. It became a national monument in 1937 and a national park in 1971.

**Good to know:** Most visitors stay along Highway 24 and the Scenic Drive. Cathedral Valley needs a high-clearance vehicle.

## Carlsbad Caverns National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | New Mexico |
| **Became a national park** | May 14, 1930 (national monument from 1923) |
| **Size** | about 46,800 acres (73 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 411,000 (46th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **The Big Room:** One of the largest cave chambers in North America, with a 1.25-mile trail.
- **Natural Entrance:** A steep trail that walks you down into the cave the way early explorers went.
- **Bat flights:** Hundreds of thousands of Brazilian free-tailed bats leave the cave at dusk from late May to October.
- **Over 119 caves:** Most were dissolved out of an ancient reef by sulfuric acid, which is unusual for caves.

**History:** The Mescalero Apache knew the cave entrance long before settlers arrived. A teenage cowboy, Jim White, began exploring it around 1898 and later led tours, while miners dug up bat guano to sell as fertilizer. It became a national monument in 1923 and a national park in 1930. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

**Good to know:** Cave entry needs a timed reservation. An elevator also connects the visitor center to the Big Room.

## Grand Canyon National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Arizona |
| **Became a national park** | February 26, 1919 (national monument from 1908) |
| **Size** | about 1.20 million acres (1,880 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 4.43 million (4th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **The canyon:** 277 river miles long, up to 18 miles wide and about a mile deep.
- **South Rim:** Mather Point, the Yavapai Geology Museum and Desert View Watchtower, open all year.
- **Hiking and rafting:** Trails such as Bright Angel and South Kaibab lead toward the Colorado River; river trips take 1 to 3 weeks.
- **North Rim:** Higher, cooler and quieter. It reopened in May 2026 after a 2025 wildfire, but without its historic lodge.

**History:** Eleven tribes, including the Havasupai, Hualapai, Navajo, Hopi, Paiute and Zuni, have deep ties to the canyon. Spanish explorers reached the rim in 1540, and John Wesley Powell led the first known river trip through it in 1869. President Theodore Roosevelt said "Leave it as it is" in 1903 and made it a national monument in 1908; it became a national park in 1919. In July 2025 the Dragon Bravo Fire burned about 145,000 acres and destroyed the historic Grand Canyon Lodge on the North Rim.

**Good to know:** The North Rim is open mid-May to mid-October with limited services. Havasu Falls is on the Havasupai Reservation, not in the park.

## Great Basin National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Nevada |
| **Became a national park** | October 27, 1986 |
| **Size** | about 77,200 acres (120 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 161,000 (54th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Bristlecone pines:** Some of the oldest trees on Earth, nearly 5,000 years old.
- **Wheeler Peak:** A 13,063-ft peak with a small glacier and a scenic drive to 10,000 ft.
- **Lehman Caves:** A marble cave full of rare shield formations.
- **Dark skies:** One of the darkest night skies in the US.

**History:** The Fremont people and later the Western Shoshone and Goshute lived in the area. Rancher Absalom Lehman discovered the caves around 1885, and they became a national monument in 1922. In 1964 a bristlecone pine about 4,900 years old, later named Prometheus, was cut down for study, which helped build support for the park, created in 1986.

**Good to know:** The park is remote, about 5 hours from Las Vegas or Salt Lake City. The nearest town is tiny Baker, Nevada.

## Guadalupe Mountains National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Texas |
| **Became a national park** | September 30, 1972 (authorized by Congress in 1966) |
| **Size** | about 86,400 acres (140 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 206,000 (52nd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Guadalupe Peak:** The highest point in Texas (8,751 ft).
- **El Capitan:** A sheer limestone cliff that rises above the desert.
- **Fossil reef:** One of the best examples of an ancient fossil reef, from the Permian period about 265 million years ago.
- **McKittrick Canyon:** Known for bright fall colors in a desert setting.

**History:** The Mescalero Apache lived in these mountains until the 1880s. The Butterfield Overland Mail stagecoach line passed by in 1858, and its Pinery station ruins remain. Geologist Wallace Pratt donated land in McKittrick Canyon, and the park was established in 1972.

**Good to know:** There is no gas, food or lodging in the park, and strong winds are common.

## Petrified Forest National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Arizona |
| **Became a national park** | December 9, 1962 (national monument from 1906) |
| **Size** | about 221,000 acres (350 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 316,000 (48th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Petrified wood:** Logs from the Late Triassic period, about 218 million years ago, turned to colorful quartz.
- **Painted Desert:** Badlands striped in red, pink and purple.
- **Newspaper Rock and Puerco Pueblo:** Hundreds of petroglyphs and the ruins of an ancient village.
- **Triassic fossils:** Fossils of early dinosaurs and other reptiles, including Chindesaurus from our Triassic dinosaur list.

**History:** People have lived in the area for about 13,000 years. By the late 1800s, souvenir collectors and gem hunters were hauling away the petrified wood, so President Theodore Roosevelt made it a national monument in 1906 to protect it. It became a national park in 1962. It is the only national park that protects a section of historic Route 66.

**Good to know:** Taking any petrified wood is illegal. The park is right off Interstate 40.

## Saguaro National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Arizona |
| **Became a national park** | October 14, 1994 (national monument from 1933) |
| **Size** | about 92,900 acres (150 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 848,000 (28th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Giant saguaros:** Cactuses that can grow over 40 ft tall and live 150 years or more, found only in the Sonoran Desert.
- **Two districts:** The Rincon Mountain District east of Tucson and the Tucson Mountain District to the west.
- **Signal Hill:** Hohokam petroglyphs more than 800 years old.
- **Sky islands:** Trails climb from desert into pine forest in the Rincon Mountains.

**History:** The Tohono O'odham have harvested saguaro fruit for generations and mark their new year with the harvest. Scientists from the University of Arizona pushed to protect the cactus forest, and President Herbert Hoover made it a national monument in 1933. It became a national park in 1994.

**Good to know:** Best from October to April. Saguaros bloom in May and June.

## White Sands National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | New Mexico |
| **Became a national park** | December 20, 2019 (national monument from 1933) |
| **Size** | about 146,000 acres (230 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 660,000 (35th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Gypsum dunes:** The largest gypsum dunefield on Earth, with bright white sand that stays cool to the touch.
- **Sledding:** Sledding down the dunes on plastic saucers sold at the visitor center.
- **Dunes Drive and trails:** A scenic road and trails including the Interdune Boardwalk and the Alkali Flat Trail.
- **Ancient footprints:** Fossil human footprints about 21,000–23,000 years old, among the oldest evidence of people in the Americas.

**History:** The dunes formed as an ancient lake dried up and wind blew its gypsum crystals into dunes. The Mescalero Apache lived in the region. President Herbert Hoover made it a national monument in 1933, and it became the 62nd national park in 2019. It is surrounded by the White Sands Missile Range, where the first atomic bomb was tested in 1945.

**Good to know:** The park and nearby Highway 70 can close for a few hours during missile tests.

## Zion National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Utah |
| **Became a national park** | November 19, 1919 (Mukuntuweap National Monument from 1909) |
| **Size** | about 147,200 acres (230 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 4.98 million (2nd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Zion Canyon:** Towering red and cream cliffs along the Virgin River.
- **Angels Landing:** A famous, exposed hike along a narrow ridge. A permit from a lottery has been required since 2022.
- **The Narrows:** Wading upstream through a slot canyon in the Virgin River.
- **Zion–Mount Carmel Highway:** A scenic road with a 1.1-mile tunnel completed in 1930.

**History:** Ancestral Puebloans and later the Southern Paiute lived in the canyon. Mormon settlers arrived in the 1860s and named it Zion. It became Mukuntuweap National Monument in 1909, was renamed Zion in 1918, and became Utah's first national park in 1919. It is now the second most visited national park.

**Good to know:** Most of the year, a free shuttle is the only way to drive up Zion Canyon. Check for flash-flood danger before hiking the Narrows.
