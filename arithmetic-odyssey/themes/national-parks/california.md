## Channel Islands National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | March 5, 1980 (national monument from 1938) |
| **Size** | about 250,000 acres (390 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 227,000 (51st most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Five islands:** Anacapa, Santa Cruz, Santa Rosa, San Miguel and Santa Barbara, each with its own character. Half the park is ocean.
- **Island fox:** A small fox found nowhere else on Earth, saved from near extinction in the 2000s.
- **Sea caves and kayaking:** Painted Cave on Santa Cruz Island is one of the largest sea caves in the world.
- **Ocean life:** Kelp forests for snorkeling and diving, and whales and dolphins on the boat crossing.

**History:** The Chumash and Tongva peoples lived on and paddled between the islands in plank canoes. Remains found on Santa Rosa Island ("Arlington Springs Man") are about 13,000 years old, among the oldest human remains in North America. After a century of ranching, the islands became a national monument in 1938 and a national park in 1980. The islands are nicknamed the "Galápagos of North America" for their unique species.

**Good to know:** Boats leave from Ventura and Oxnard. There is no food, water or lodging on the islands.

## Death Valley National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California, Nevada |
| **Became a national park** | October 31, 1994 (national monument from 1933) |
| **Size** | about 3.41 million acres (5,320 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.32 million (24th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Badwater Basin:** Salt flats at 282 ft below sea level, the lowest point in North America.
- **Extreme heat:** The hottest air temperature ever recorded on Earth: 134°F (56.7°C) at Furnace Creek in 1913.
- **Viewpoints:** Zabriskie Point, Dante's View and the colored hills of Artists Palette.
- **Dunes and the Racetrack:** Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes and Racetrack Playa, where rocks slide across the dry lakebed on thin ice.

**History:** The Timbisha Shoshone have lived here for centuries and still have a village at Furnace Creek. The name comes from gold-rush travelers who got lost crossing the valley in 1849. Borax mining in the 1880s made the valley famous for its "20-mule teams." It became a national monument in 1933 and a national park in 1994 under the California Desert Protection Act. It is the largest national park outside Alaska.

**Good to know:** Visit from November to March. Summer heat is dangerous; carry plenty of water.

## Joshua Tree National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | October 31, 1994 (national monument from 1936) |
| **Size** | about 795,000 acres (1,240 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 2.93 million (12th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Joshua trees:** The twisted yucca trees of the Mojave Desert that give the park its name.
- **Two deserts:** The higher, cooler Mojave and the lower, hotter Colorado Desert meet inside the park.
- **Rock climbing:** Giant piles of granite boulders make it one of the world's top climbing destinations.
- **Night skies:** An International Dark Sky Park, great for stargazing.

**History:** The Cahuilla, Serrano, Chemehuevi and Mojave peoples lived here. Pasadena resident Minerva Hoyt campaigned to protect the desert from cactus poachers, and President Franklin Roosevelt created the national monument in 1936. It became a national park in 1994 under the California Desert Protection Act.

**Good to know:** Best from October to May. There is very little water in the park; bring your own.

## Kings Canyon National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | March 4, 1940 (took in General Grant National Park, created in 1890) |
| **Size** | about 462,000 acres (720 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 780,000 (30th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **General Grant Tree:** One of the largest trees in the world, named the "Nation's Christmas Tree."
- **Kings Canyon:** One of the deepest canyons in the United States, over 8,000 ft deep in places.
- **Cedar Grove and Zumwalt Meadow:** A glacier-carved valley floor with meadows, river trails and granite walls.
- **High Sierra backcountry:** Trails such as the Rae Lakes Loop lead into some of the wildest country in the Sierra Nevada.

**History:** The Mono and Yokuts peoples lived and traded here. General Grant National Park was created in 1890 to protect the Grant Grove of giant sequoias, and in 1940 it was absorbed into the new, much larger Kings Canyon National Park. Since 1943 Kings Canyon and Sequoia have been managed together.

**Good to know:** The road down to Cedar Grove is usually open only from late spring to fall.

## Lassen Volcanic National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | August 9, 1916 |
| **Size** | about 106,000 acres (170 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 505,000 (40th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Lassen Peak:** One of the largest plug-dome volcanoes in the world (10,457 ft), with a summit trail.
- **All four volcano types:** Plug dome, shield, cinder cone and composite volcanoes all appear in the park.
- **Bumpass Hell:** Boiling mud pots, steaming vents and hot pools along a boardwalk.
- **Manzanita Lake:** A lake with views of Lassen Peak, popular for kayaking and photography.

**History:** The Atsugewi, Yana, Yahi and Maidu peoples lived around the mountain. Lassen Peak erupted from 1914 to 1917, including a huge blast in May 1915, and this helped convince Congress to create the park in 1916. It was one of the few eruptions in the contiguous US before Mount St. Helens in 1980.

**Good to know:** The main park highway is buried in deep snow most of the year and usually opens fully in June or July.

## Pinnacles National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | January 10, 2013 (national monument from 1908) |
| **Size** | about 26,700 acres (42 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 343,000 (47th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Rock spires:** The remains of an ancient volcano that the San Andreas Fault has carried about 195 miles from where it formed.
- **Talus caves:** Bear Gulch and Balconies caves, formed by boulders wedged in narrow canyons. Bring a flashlight.
- **California condors:** One of the places where the endangered condor has been released back into the wild.
- **High Peaks Trail:** A trail with narrow stone steps carved into the rock among the spires.

**History:** The Chalon and Mutsun Ohlone peoples lived in the area. Homesteader Schuyler Hain led tours and campaigned to protect the rocks, and President Theodore Roosevelt made it a national monument in 1908. It became a national park in 2013.

**Good to know:** No road crosses the park; the east and west entrances are not connected. Spring has wildflowers; summers are very hot.

## Redwood National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | October 2, 1968 (managed together with three California state parks since 1994) |
| **Size** | about 139,000 acres (220 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.20 million (25th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **The tallest trees on Earth:** Coast redwoods over 350 ft tall. The tallest known tree, Hyperion (about 380 ft), is kept in a secret location.
- **Fern Canyon:** A narrow canyon with walls covered in ferns, used as a filming location for "The Lost World: Jurassic Park."
- **Groves:** Lady Bird Johnson Grove and the Tall Trees Grove.
- **Roosevelt elk:** Herds of elk graze in the meadows and along the beaches.

**History:** The Yurok, Tolowa and Chilula peoples have lived among the redwoods for thousands of years. Since the 1850s, about 95% of the original old-growth redwood forest has been logged. The Save the Redwoods League (founded 1918) protected groves in state parks, and Congress created the national park in 1968 and expanded it in 1978. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

**Good to know:** The national park itself charges no entrance fee. In summer, a parking permit is needed for Fern Canyon.

## Sequoia National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | September 25, 1890 |
| **Size** | about 404,000 acres (630 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.38 million (23rd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **General Sherman Tree:** The largest tree on Earth by volume.
- **Giant Forest:** A grove with thousands of giant sequoias and the Congress Trail.
- **Moro Rock:** A granite dome with about 350 steps to the top and views across the Sierra.
- **Mount Whitney and Crystal Cave:** The highest peak in the contiguous US (14,505 ft) is on the park's eastern edge; Crystal Cave is a marble cave with tours.

**History:** The Western Mono (Monache) and Yokuts peoples lived in the foothills. Sequoia was the second national park created in the US, in 1890, to protect the giant sequoias from logging. Wildfires in 2020 and 2021 killed up to about a fifth of the world's large giant sequoias, and the park now works to protect the remaining groves.

**Good to know:** The Generals Highway is steep and winding; long vehicles are advised not to use the section near the Foothills.

## Yosemite National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | California |
| **Became a national park** | October 1, 1890 (Yosemite Valley protected by the Yosemite Grant of 1864) |
| **Size** | about 761,000 acres (1,190 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 4.28 million (5th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Yosemite Valley:** El Capitan, Half Dome and Yosemite Falls (2,425 ft), one of the tallest waterfalls in North America.
- **Glacier Point:** A viewpoint over the valley and Half Dome.
- **Mariposa Grove:** More than 500 giant sequoias.
- **Tuolumne Meadows:** High-country meadows and granite domes along the Tioga Road.

**History:** Yosemite Valley is the homeland of the Ahwahneechee and other Southern Sierra Miwok peoples, who were driven out by the Mariposa Battalion in 1851. In 1864 President Lincoln signed the Yosemite Grant, the first time the federal government set land aside for public enjoyment. John Muir's writing helped create the national park in 1890, and he founded the Sierra Club in 1892. The damming of the park's Hetch Hetchy Valley in the 1910s became one of the first great American conservation battles.

**Good to know:** Summer is very busy, and reservations to enter have been needed at peak times in recent years. Check the park website before visiting.
