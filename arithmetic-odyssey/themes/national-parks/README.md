# US National Parks

A guide to all **63 US national parks**: where they are, when they became national parks, how big they are, how many people visit, their key attractions and a short history of each.

## Coming soon: a 64th park

**Chiricahua, Arizona.** Chiricahua National Monument (protected since 1924) is set to become the 64th national park and Arizona's fourth. The House passed the Chiricahua National Park Act in March 2026 and the Senate on September 30, 2026. As of mid-October 2026 it was awaiting the President's signature. The "Wonderland of Rocks": thousands of rock pinnacles and balanced rocks formed from volcanic ash about 27 million years ago, plus the historic Faraway Ranch. The Chiricahua Mountains are the homeland of the Chiricahua Apache, led by Cochise and Geronimo in the 1860s–1880s.

## Quick facts

- **Oldest:** Yellowstone National Park, March 1, 1872, the first national park in the world.
- **Newest:** New River Gorge National Park, December 27, 2020.
- **Largest:** Wrangell–St. Elias National Park, about 8.32 million acres.
- **Smallest:** Gateway Arch National Park, 91 acres.
- **Most visited in 2025:** Great Smoky Mountains National Park, 11.53 million visits, more than twice as many as any other park.
- **Least visited in 2025:** Kobuk Valley National Park, 7,786 visits.
- **States with the most parks:** California (9), Alaska (8), Utah (5).
- **Total visits to all 63 parks in 2025:** about 93.9 million.

## Sources and notes

- Visitor numbers are the National Park Service's 2025 recreation visit counts, released in March 2026 ([NPS announcement](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1207/03-13-26-2025-visitation-statsitics.htm), [park-by-park figures](https://publiclandsdata.com/national-park-visitation/rankings)). Numbers under 1 million are rounded to the nearest thousand.
- Sizes are approximate National Park Service figures for the national park itself. Several Alaska parks, Great Sand Dunes and New River Gorge also have a "preserve" next to the park, where hunting is allowed, that is not included.
- Dates are when each place became a national park. Many were protected earlier as national monuments or other types of park, which is noted on each park's page.
- Conditions change: roads close for snow, fires and floods; some parks need reservations in the busy season. Check each park's page on [nps.gov](https://www.nps.gov) before visiting.
- Facts are current as of October 2026.
