## Badlands National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | South Dakota |
| **Became a national park** | November 10, 1978 (national monument from 1939) |
| **Size** | about 242,800 acres (380 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 1.14 million (26th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Eroded buttes and spires:** Layered, striped rock formations along the Badlands Loop Road.
- **Fossils:** One of the richest fossil beds in the world for ancient mammals, such as early horses, rhinos and saber-toothed cats. You can watch paleontologists at work in the Fossil Prep Lab.
- **Prairie wildlife:** Bison, bighorn sheep, prairie dogs and the endangered black-footed ferret.
- **Trails:** Short hikes such as the Notch Trail, with a log ladder.

**History:** The Lakota called the area "mako sica," meaning "land bad." Ghost Dance ceremonies were held on Stronghold Table in 1890, shortly before the Wounded Knee Massacre. It became a national monument in 1939 and a national park in 1978. The park's South Unit is on the Pine Ridge Reservation and is managed with the Oglala Lakota.

**Good to know:** Mount Rushmore is about 1.5 hours away. Summer storms can be sudden and strong.

## Black Canyon of the Gunnison National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Colorado |
| **Became a national park** | October 21, 1999 (national monument from 1933) |
| **Size** | about 30,800 acres (48 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 250,000 (50th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **A deep, narrow canyon:** Walls so steep that parts of the canyon floor get only about 33 minutes of sunlight a day.
- **Painted Wall:** The tallest cliff in Colorado (2,250 ft), streaked with pale rock.
- **South Rim Road:** A 7-mile drive with a dozen overlooks.
- **Inner canyon routes:** Steep, unmaintained routes down to the river for experienced hikers, plus world-class rock climbing.

**History:** The Ute people lived in the area but rarely entered the canyon. In 1901 Abraham Lincoln Fellows and William Torrence floated through it on rubber mattresses while surveying for the Gunnison Tunnel, which since 1909 has carried water to farms in the Uncompahgre Valley. It became a national monument in 1933 and a national park in 1999.

**Good to know:** The North Rim road closes in winter. The South Rim is closer to the town of Montrose.

## Glacier National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Montana |
| **Became a national park** | May 11, 1910 |
| **Size** | about 1.01 million acres (1,580 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 3.14 million (10th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Going-to-the-Sun Road:** A 50-mile mountain road over Logan Pass, a National Historic Landmark.
- **Many Glacier:** Lakes, peaks and trails to Grinnell Glacier and Iceberg Lake.
- **Lake McDonald:** The largest lake in the park, known for its colorful stones.
- **Glaciers and wildlife:** About 26 named glaciers remain, down from about 150 in 1850. Mountain goats, bighorn sheep and grizzly bears live here.

**History:** The Blackfeet, Salish, Kootenai and Pend d'Oreille peoples have lived here for thousands of years. Conservationist George Bird Grinnell called it the "Crown of the Continent" and campaigned for the park, created in 1910. The Great Northern Railway then built hotels and chalets to bring in tourists. In 1932 it joined Canada's Waterton Lakes to form the world's first International Peace Park.

**Good to know:** Going-to-the-Sun Road is usually fully open only from late June or July to October. Vehicle reservations have been needed in recent summers.

## Grand Teton National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Wyoming |
| **Became a national park** | February 26, 1929 (expanded in 1950) |
| **Size** | about 310,000 acres (480 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 3.80 million (8th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Teton Range:** Peaks that rise about 7,000 ft straight up from the valley floor, with no foothills. Grand Teton is 13,775 ft.
- **Jenny Lake:** A boat ride across the lake to Hidden Falls and Cascade Canyon.
- **Classic views:** Oxbow Bend, Schwabacher Landing and the historic barns on Mormon Row.
- **Wildlife:** Moose, bison, elk, pronghorn and bears.

**History:** The Shoshone, Bannock, Crow, Blackfeet and other peoples used the valley in summer. French trappers named the peaks "les trois tétons." The first park in 1929 protected only the mountains. John D. Rockefeller Jr. secretly bought valley land, and President Franklin Roosevelt's 1943 Jackson Hole National Monument caused a fierce fight with ranchers before the land was merged into the park in 1950.

**Good to know:** Yellowstone is just 10 miles to the north, so many people visit both on one trip.

## Great Sand Dunes National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Colorado |
| **Became a national park** | September 13, 2004 (national monument from 1932) |
| **Size** | about 107,000 acres (170 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 432,000 (44th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Tallest dunes in North America:** Star Dune rises about 750 ft.
- **Sandboarding and sledding:** Boards and sleds can be rented outside the park.
- **Medano Creek:** A seasonal creek in late spring with waves that pulse across the sand, like a beach in the mountains.
- **Alpine preserve:** Peaks, forests and lakes in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains.

**History:** The Ute and Jicarilla Apache peoples lived in and passed through the San Luis Valley. Explorer Zebulon Pike crossed the dunes in 1807. President Herbert Hoover made it a national monument in 1932, and it became a national park and preserve in 2004.

**Good to know:** On summer afternoons the sand can reach 150°F (66°C). Go early in the morning or in the evening.

## Mesa Verde National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Colorado |
| **Became a national park** | June 29, 1906 |
| **Size** | about 52,500 acres (82 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 463,000 (43rd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Cliff Palace:** The largest cliff dwelling in North America, with about 150 rooms.
- **Balcony House and Long House:** Cliff dwellings reached on ranger-guided tours with ladders and tunnels.
- **Mesa Top Loop:** A drive past pit houses, villages and canyon viewpoints.
- **About 5,000 sites:** Including around 600 cliff dwellings.

**History:** Ancestral Puebloans lived here from about 550 to 1300 CE and built the cliff dwellings in the 1200s. They moved south around 1300, probably because of long droughts; today's Pueblo peoples are their descendants. Ranchers Richard Wetherill and Charlie Mason came across Cliff Palace in 1888, and looting of the sites led to the park in 1906. It was the first national park created to protect the works of people, and it was among the first UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

**Good to know:** Tickets for the ranger-guided cliff dwelling tours sell out in summer.

## Rocky Mountain National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Colorado |
| **Became a national park** | January 26, 1915 |
| **Size** | about 265,800 acres (420 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 4.17 million (6th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Trail Ridge Road:** The highest continuous paved road in the US, reaching 12,183 ft.
- **Lakes and peaks:** Bear Lake, Dream Lake and Emerald Lake, and more than 60 peaks over 12,000 ft, including Longs Peak (14,259 ft).
- **Elk:** Herds of elk; in fall, the bulls' bugling echoes across Moraine Park.
- **Alpine tundra and the Continental Divide:** Treeless tundra high above the forest, and the headwaters of the Colorado River.

**History:** The Ute and Arapaho peoples hunted and traveled through these mountains. Naturalist Enos Mills, known as the "father of Rocky Mountain National Park," campaigned for years to protect it, and it became a national park in 1915.

**Good to know:** Timed-entry reservations have been needed in summer and early fall in recent years. Trail Ridge Road closes in winter.

## Theodore Roosevelt National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | North Dakota |
| **Became a national park** | November 10, 1978 (Theodore Roosevelt National Memorial Park from 1947) |
| **Size** | about 70,400 acres (110 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 730,000 (33rd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Three units:** The South Unit by Medora, the quieter North Unit, and the remote Elkhorn Ranch, the site of Roosevelt's ranch.
- **Badlands:** Colorful, eroded hills along the Little Missouri River.
- **Wildlife:** Bison, wild horses, elk and prairie dog towns.
- **Roosevelt's cabin:** The Maltese Cross Cabin, where Roosevelt stayed in the 1880s, at the South Unit visitor center.

**History:** Theodore Roosevelt first came here in 1883 to hunt bison and started two cattle ranches. After his mother and wife died on the same day in 1884, he returned to the badlands to grieve. He later said, "I never would have been President if it had not been for my experiences in North Dakota." The area became a memorial park in 1947 and a national park in 1978. It is the only national park named directly after a person.

**Good to know:** Interstate 94 runs past the South Unit at Medora. The North Unit is about 70 miles north.

## Wind Cave National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | South Dakota |
| **Became a national park** | January 9, 1903 |
| **Size** | about 34,000 acres (53 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 606,000 (37th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Wind Cave:** One of the longest caves in the world, with about 167 miles of mapped passages.
- **Boxwork:** Thin, honeycomb-like formations; Wind Cave has most of the world's known boxwork.
- **A breathing cave:** Wind blows in or out of the entrance as the air pressure outside changes.
- **Prairie above:** Bison, elk, pronghorn and prairie dogs on the grasslands above the cave.

**History:** In Lakota tradition, the first people emerged onto the Earth from Wind Cave, and it is a sacred place. Brothers Tom and Jesse Bingham noticed wind whistling out of a small hole in 1881, and teenager Alvin McDonald explored and mapped miles of the cave in the 1890s. President Theodore Roosevelt made it a national park in 1903, the first cave in the world to become a national park.

**Good to know:** The cave can be seen only on ranger-led tours. Check tour availability before you go.

## Yellowstone National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Wyoming, Montana, Idaho |
| **Became a national park** | March 1, 1872 |
| **Size** | about 2.22 million acres (3,470 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 4.76 million (3rd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Geysers and hot springs:** More than half of the world's geysers, including Old Faithful, and the rainbow-colored Grand Prismatic Spring.
- **Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone:** A yellow-walled canyon with the 308-ft Lower Falls.
- **Wildlife:** Bison, elk, grizzly bears and wolves in the Lamar and Hayden valleys.
- **Mammoth Hot Springs and Yellowstone Lake:** Steaming travertine terraces, and the largest high-elevation lake in North America.

**History:** At least 27 tribes have ties to Yellowstone. After explorers described its wonders in 1870 and 1871, helped by Thomas Moran's paintings and William Henry Jackson's photographs, President Ulysses S. Grant signed it into law in 1872 as the first national park in the world. The US Army ran the park until 1918. Wolves were brought back in 1995, and a huge flood in 2022 washed out roads in the north. The park sits on top of a giant volcanic caldera.

**Good to know:** Most roads close from November to April. Stay on boardwalks in thermal areas; the water can be boiling.
