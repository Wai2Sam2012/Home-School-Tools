## Cuyahoga Valley National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Ohio |
| **Became a national park** | October 11, 2000 (national recreation area from 1974) |
| **Size** | about 32,600 acres (51 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 3.03 million (11th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Brandywine Falls:** A 60-ft waterfall with a boardwalk.
- **Towpath Trail:** A bike and walking trail along the old Ohio & Erie Canal.
- **Scenic railroad:** The Cuyahoga Valley Scenic Railroad, which you can ride one way and bike back.
- **The Ledges and Beaver Marsh:** Sandstone cliffs, and a marsh full of wildlife.

**History:** The Ohio & Erie Canal, opened in 1827, ran through the valley and helped Ohio grow. In 1969 the polluted Cuyahoga River caught fire in Cleveland; the national attention helped lead to the Clean Water Act and the EPA. The valley became a national recreation area in 1974 and a national park in 2000, protecting green space between Cleveland and Akron.

**Good to know:** There is no entrance fee. The park is open all year.

## Gateway Arch National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Missouri |
| **Became a national park** | February 22, 2018 (Jefferson National Expansion Memorial from 1935) |
| **Size** | 91 acres (0.14 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 2.21 million (15th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **The Gateway Arch:** At 630 ft, the tallest arch in the world.
- **Tram to the top:** Small pod-shaped tram cars ride up inside the arch to a viewing area.
- **Museum at the Gateway Arch:** Exhibits on westward expansion and the history of St. Louis.
- **Old Courthouse:** Where Dred Scott sued for his freedom in the 1840s and 1850s.

**History:** The memorial was created in 1935 to honor Thomas Jefferson, the Louisiana Purchase and westward expansion. Architect Eero Saarinen won a design contest in 1947, and the arch was built between 1963 and 1965. It was renamed and became a national park in 2018. It is the smallest national park.

**Good to know:** Tram tickets often sell out on busy days, so buy them ahead. The park grounds are free.

## Indiana Dunes National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Indiana |
| **Became a national park** | February 15, 2019 (national lakeshore from 1966) |
| **Size** | about 15,400 acres (24 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | 2.63 million (13th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Lake Michigan beaches:** 15 miles of shoreline with sandy beaches, including West Beach.
- **Dunes and Mount Baldy:** Tall sand dunes, including a dune that moves inland several feet a year.
- **Cowles Bog:** Where Henry Cowles' studies of plants changing over time helped found the science of ecology.
- **Plant diversity:** More than 1,100 native plant species, one of the highest counts of any national park.

**History:** The Potawatomi people lived along the lake. Botanist Henry Cowles studied how plant life changes across the dunes in the late 1890s. As steel mills and ports spread along the shore, Senator Paul Douglas and local activists fought to save the dunes, and Congress created a national lakeshore in 1966. It became the 61st national park in 2019.

**Good to know:** The South Shore Line train from Chicago stops near the park. Indiana Dunes State Park, with the 3 Dune Challenge, is inside the national park but run separately.

## Isle Royale National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Michigan |
| **Became a national park** | April 3, 1940 (authorized by Congress in 1931) |
| **Size** | about 571,800 acres (890 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 29,000 (60th most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Wolves and moose:** The world's longest-running study of predators and prey, going on since 1958.
- **Backpacking:** The 40-mile Greenstone Ridge Trail across the island.
- **Kayaking and canoeing:** Calm inland lakes and sheltered harbors.
- **Shipwrecks:** Ten major shipwrecks in the cold, clear water of Lake Superior.

**History:** Native peoples mined copper on the island thousands of years ago; the Ojibwe call it Minong. Later came commercial fishing and resorts. It became a national park in 1940. When the wolf population dropped to just two in the 2010s, new wolves were brought to the island in 2018 and 2019. It is the least visited national park in the lower 48 states.

**Good to know:** The island is reached only by ferry or seaplane, and the park closes completely from November to mid-April.

## Voyageurs National Park

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Minnesota |
| **Became a national park** | April 8, 1975 (authorized by Congress in 1971) |
| **Size** | about 218,200 acres (340 sq mi) |
| **Visitors in 2025** | about 206,000 (53rd most visited of 63) |

**Key attractions**

- **Lakes:** A water-based park on Rainy, Kabetogama, Namakan and Sand Point lakes, along the Canadian border.
- **Houseboats and camping:** Houseboat rentals and campsites reachable only by boat.
- **Kettle Falls Hotel:** A historic hotel from the 1910s, reachable only by water.
- **Northern lights and winter:** Dark skies for the aurora, plus ice roads, snowmobiling and snowshoeing in winter.

**History:** The Ojibwe and earlier peoples lived here for thousands of years. From the late 1600s, French-Canadian fur traders called voyageurs paddled canoes through these waters, and the route later became part of the US–Canada border. After logging, gold mining and commercial fishing, the park was established in 1975.

**Good to know:** Most of the park can be reached only by boat in summer, or over the ice in winter.
